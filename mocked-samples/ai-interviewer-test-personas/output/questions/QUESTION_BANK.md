# Interviewer question bank

36 roles. 180 core questions (always asked) and 360 role follow-ups (asked only when a trigger fires). Draft for review; see README.md for how to use it.

Interview order: shared opening, five core questions, shared closing.

- Opening: To start, please tell me a little about your background and what interests you in this kind of work.
- Closing: Before we wrap up, is there anything about your experience you would like to add that we have not covered?

## Retail

### RET-01: Store Associate (grocery) (entry)

**1. Motivation.** What draws you to working in a grocery store?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate gives general reasons for liking store work but names no moment or experience that sparked the interest.
  Ask: Can you think of a moment, at work or elsewhere, that showed you this was work you enjoy?
- *mentions_customer* (priority 2). When: The candidate says they enjoy helping or chatting with shoppers but does not describe what that help looks like.
  Ask: What does helping a shopper well look like to you in a store?

**2. Experience.** Tell me about a time you kept things organized and fresh, such as shelves, a pantry or supplies, at work, at school, or anywhere else.

- *no_personal_role* (priority 1). When: The candidate explains how a team or group kept things organized and fresh but does not say which tasks they personally carried out.
  Ask: Which of those steps did you personally handle?
- *skips_safety_or_compliance_step* (priority 2). When: The candidate describes keeping things organized but never mentions checking dates or removing out-of-date or damaged items.
  Ask: What would you do if you found an item past its date?

**3. Behavioral.** Tell me about a time things got very busy and you had to stay accurate while keeping people moving, at work, at school, or anywhere else.

- *no_outcome* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes working through a busy time but never says how it ended.
  Ask: How did that busy time end?
- *mentions_conflict* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions an impatient or upset person during the busy time.
  Ask: How did you deal with the upset person?

**4. Scenario.** Suppose a shopper asks where an item is while you are in the middle of stocking a full cart. What would you do?

- *unclear_answer* (priority 1). When: The candidate's plan for the shopper and the cart is ambiguous or contradicts itself, so what they would do first is unclear.
  Ask: To be sure I follow, which would you do first?
- *mentions_time_pressure* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions competing demands such as stocking deadlines, a busy floor or several people needing help at once.
  Ask: How would you decide when the stocking can wait and when it cannot?

**5. Growth.** How would you get up to speed on the part of grocery work you know least?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate names a way they would learn but gives no example of picking up a new task or area before.
  Ask: Can you share an example of learning a new task at work, school or elsewhere?
- *strong_answer_stretch* (priority 3). When: The candidate gives a clear learning plan backed by a specific past example with a good result.
  Ask: Suppose you were moved to a new department on short notice. How would you get comfortable there?

### RET-02: Customer Service Representative (online retail) (mid)

**1. Motivation.** What keeps you drawn to customer support in online retail?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes liking support work in general terms without naming a particular case or experience that shaped that interest.
  Ask: Is there a particular customer case or moment that shaped your interest in this work?
- *mentions_leadership* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions coaching, guiding or developing teammates as part of what draws them.
  Ask: What does helping a teammate get better at the job look like to you?

**2. Experience.** Walk me through a template, process or policy you improved so that customers had to contact support less often.

- *no_measure* (priority 2). When: The candidate describes an improvement to support content or process but gives no sense of how big its effect was or how it was checked.
  Ask: Roughly how big was the effect of that change?
- *mentions_tool_or_method* (priority 1). When: The candidate names a ticketing tool, report or method used to spot which contacts were repeating.
  Ask: How did you find out which contacts were repeating in the first place?

**3. Behavioral.** Describe a time you handled an escalated customer who was very upset when policy could not give them what they wanted.

- *no_outcome* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes the escalated case and their approach but never says how the customer or the case ended up.
  Ask: How did that case end?
- *mentions_mistake* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions that an error, by the company or by someone on the team, caused the customer's frustration.
  Ask: What did you do about the error itself?

**4. Scenario.** Suppose you lead a pod and a former peer keeps missing quality targets. How would you approach the first conversation?

- *unclear_answer* (priority 1). When: The candidate's plan for the conversation is vague or contradicts itself, so what they would actually say or do is unclear.
  Ask: What would you actually say in that first conversation, in your own words?
- *mentions_conflict* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions friction, pushback or awkwardness that could come from managing someone who used to be a peer.
  Ask: How would you keep working trust intact while still holding the standard?

**5. Growth.** How would you build the skill you most need to lead a support pod well?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate names a skill and a plan but gives no example of building a skill like this before.
  Ask: Can you share a time you built a new work skill?
- *strong_answer_stretch* (priority 3). When: The candidate gives a clear, specific development plan tied to real past experience and results.
  Ask: Suppose you had to plan weekend queue coverage with very little history. What would you do?

### RET-03: Assistant Store Manager (mid)

**1. Motivation.** What draws you to running a retail store as a manager rather than staying on the sales floor?

- *no_measure* (priority 2). When: The candidate describes the steps taken to reduce loss or errors but gives no figures or scale for how much it changed.
  Ask: Roughly how big was that change?
- *mentions_tool_or_method* (priority 1). When: The candidate names a specific count, audit, receiving check or report used to cut losses.
  Ask: How did you keep that practice going week after week?

**3. Behavioral.** Describe a time you had to address an associate's repeated performance problems while keeping the team steady.

- *no_outcome* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes the conversations with the associate but never says whether performance or the team's morale changed afterward.
  Ask: What happened afterward, for that associate and the team?
- *mentions_conflict* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions pushback, defensiveness or tension from the associate.
  Ask: How did you respond to the associate's reaction?

**4. Scenario.** Suppose sales are behind plan and labor hours are over budget in the same week. What would you do first?

- *unclear_answer* (priority 1). When: The candidate's plan for sales and labor is ambiguous or contradicts itself, so the order of their actions is unclear.
  Ask: To make sure I understand, what is the very first thing you would do?
- *skips_safety_or_compliance_step* (priority 2). When: The candidate proposes cutting hours or coverage without considering loss prevention, opening and closing procedures or other compliance needs.
  Ask: How would you keep loss prevention and closing procedures covered if hours were cut?

**5. Growth.** How would you get comfortable with the part of a store's profit and loss that is newest to you?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate names a budgeting gap and a plan but gives no example of working with budgets or reports before.
  Ask: Can you share a time you used a budget or sales report to make a decision?
- *strong_answer_stretch* (priority 3). When: The candidate gives a specific, realistic plan backed by past experience with budgets and targets.
  Ask: Suppose your district manager questioned a large variance in your first monthly review. How would you respond?

**1. Motivation.** What interests you about working with people's money at a bank or credit union?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate gives general reasons for the interest and names no experience from work, school or elsewhere that sparked it.
  Ask: Was there an experience at work, school or elsewhere that first sparked that interest?
- *mentions_customer* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions helping customers or members as part of what appeals to them.
  Ask: What do you enjoy most about helping people with their money?

**2. Experience.** Describe a time you kept counts or amounts accurate while things got busy, at work, at school, or anywhere else.

- *no_outcome* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes how they kept amounts accurate but never says how it turned out or what it prevented.
  Ask: How did things turn out because of that careful checking?
- *mentions_mistake* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions a counting error, mismatch or other mistake with amounts or details.
  Ask: What happened with that mistake in the end?

**3. Behavioral.** Tell me about a time you helped someone who was upset or frustrated, at work, at school, or anywhere else.

- *no_personal_role* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes the situation and what others did, and their own words or actions toward the upset person are unclear.
  Ask: What did you personally say or do for that person?
- *mentions_time_pressure* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions a long line, a busy period or other people waiting.
  Ask: How did you handle the other demands on you while that was happening?

**4. Scenario.** Suppose someone you do not know asks to cash a large check and the signature seems unusual. What would you do?

- *unclear_answer* (priority 1). When: The candidate's response is unclear about whether they would cash the check, refuse it or escalate, or it contradicts itself.
  Ask: Would you cash the check, decline it, or ask someone else to review it first?
- *skips_safety_or_compliance_step* (priority 2). When: The candidate does not mention verifying identification, following the fraud procedure or involving a supervisor before releasing cash.
  Ask: What identity checks would you complete before any cash changes hands?

**5. Growth.** Suppose your job shifted toward recommending products. What would help you get comfortable explaining account options to members?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate talks about wanting to learn product advice but gives no past example of explaining something clearly to a customer or a peer.
  Ask: Can you share a time you explained something complicated to someone in simple terms?
- *strong_answer_stretch* (priority 3). When: The candidate shows a clear, practical approach to learning products and matching them to a member's needs.
  Ask: Suppose a member's needs do not fit any product you know well. How would you proceed?

  Ask: How would you pass your skills on to newer or freelance writers?

### MED-03: Copy Editor (senior)

**1. Motivation.** What draws you to copyediting?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate speaks about loving careful editing in general terms and recalls no particular piece or manuscript.
  Ask: Can you recall a particular manuscript or piece that shows why you love careful editing?
- *mentions_improvement* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions changes they have pushed in editing practice, style rules or workflow.
  Ask: Which improvement to editing practice has mattered most to you?

**2. Experience.** Walk me through how you design a proofing process that catches errors in a long manuscript before it goes to print.

- *no_outcome* (priority 1). When: The candidate explains the design of the proofing process but not how it performed once the team used it.
  Ask: How did that process work out in practice?
- *mentions_tool_or_method* (priority 2). When: The candidate names a specific checklist, pass structure or tool used for proofing.
  Ask: How did you decide what that checklist, pass structure or tool should cover?

**3. Behavioral.** Tell me about a time an author pushed back hard on your edits. How did you handle it?

- *no_outcome* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes the author's pushback and their reply but not how the matter ended.
  Ask: How did that situation end up?
- *mentions_customer* (priority 2). When: The candidate mentions balancing the author's wishes against house style, accuracy or the publisher's needs.
  Ask: How did you balance the author's wishes against those other needs?

**4. Scenario.** Suppose a book is due at the printer tomorrow and you find a factual error late in the final pages. What would you do?

- *unclear_answer* (priority 1). When: The candidate's answer is ambiguous or contradictory about the order of steps.
  Ask: Could you clarify the order of the steps you would take?
- *skips_safety_or_compliance_step* (priority 2). When: The candidate would change the text late without verifying the fact or getting approval from the author or managing editor.
  Ask: What would you verify before making a change this late?

**5. Growth.** How do you train and develop newer editors and freelancers?

- *no_specific_example* (priority 1). When: The candidate describes their approach to developing editors in general terms without naming a particular person or occasion.
  Ask: Can you share one specific way you helped an editor improve?
- *strong_answer_stretch* (priority 2). When: The candidate gives a specific, complete account of developing editors, including methods and clear results.
  Ask: Suppose an editor resisted your guidance. How would you adjust your approach?
