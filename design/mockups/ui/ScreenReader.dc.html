Screen reader mode: one audio timeline

The browser never tells us when a screen reader has finished speaking. So a spoken status may only be sent while the interviewer’s voice is truly silent, and it is gated on a signal we own: the moment the last voice frame has actually played.

Transition Gate: the signal we wait for Screen reader hears Earcon (our audio)
Speaking → Your turn The player reports the last voice frame has left the speakers (scheduled end plus output latency), then a 250 ms guard. Never the pipeline’s “TTS done”. “Your turn” Chime, before the announcement
Your turn → Thinking Turn detector says complete. Announcements are debounced 250–500 ms and only the state still displayed is spoken, so a false cutoff says nothing new. “Thinking”, only if still current after 500 ms. Shorter flips: earcon only. Soft scribble loop, ducked
Thinking → Speaking Voice audio is ready. If an announcement was sent in the last few seconds, hold the voice for its estimated length (words ÷ the user’s reader rate). Nothing. The voice is the signal. Lead-in tone, then a 300 ms gap
Any → Paused Your key press. The voice fades out over 100 ms first, then the announcement is sent. “Paused” None
Captions Not a live region by default. A navigable transcript (finished sentences only) is the main channel for braille and deafblind users. Opt-in live captions add one final sentence at a time. Only when you navigate to them —

Two timing profiles

Natural timing matches human turn gaps. Accessible timing leaves room for screen reader speech. Accessible is the default for now; reconciling the two comes later.

Turning the mode on

Pages cannot reliably detect a screen reader. The Accessibility button is always visible, and focus lands on it first when any screen loads. Every setting has a working default.

Simulator presets

The gate reads the delayed, clipped audio the user actually hears, so it holds in perceived mode. The guard does not stretch with lag.
